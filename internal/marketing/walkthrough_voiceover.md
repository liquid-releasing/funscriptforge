# FunscriptForge — Walkthrough Voice-Over Scripts

Pure narration extracted from `walkthrough_scripts.md`. No visual cues —
just the words to read or feed into a TTS engine.

**Pacing**: ~150 words per minute is a comfortable conversational VO speed.
Both scripts target ~3 minutes, so ~450 words each.

**Pronunciation notes**:
- *FunscriptForge* — three syllables, run together: "FUN-script-forge"
- *Diglet48* — "DIG-let forty-eight"
- *Edger* — "EDGE-er"
- *funscript-tools* — "FUN-script tools" (treat as two words)
- *restim* — "REE-stim" or "REST-im" (community uses both; pick one and stay consistent)
- *Stereostim* — "STER-ee-oh-stim"
- *Tingler* — "TING-ler"
- *EstimHero* — "ES-tim HERO"
- *MultiFunPlayer* — "MULTI-fun-player"

**Tone**: conversational, confident, slightly enthusiastic. Not salesy.
You're showing a friend a tool that solves a problem.

**Pauses**: ‖ marks a beat (half-second). ‖‖ marks a longer pause (one full
beat) — usually between scenes.

## Notes for the voice talent

- **Read at conversational speed**, not announcer speed. About 150 wpm.
- **Numbers**: spell out small numbers (one, two, six). Read big numbers
  naturally ("two thousand two hundred and eleven actions" or "twenty-two
  hundred actions" — pick whichever rolls off easier).
- **Emphasis**: lean into the "before-and-after" moments and the "no
  Python, no terminal, no configuration" close.
- **Don't oversell**: this is a tool walkthrough, not a commercial. Trust
  the product to speak for itself; you're just narrating what's happening.
- **Breath**: take real breaths between sentences. Edited audio with
  consistent breaths sounds more natural than a flat read.
- **If recording yourself**: do two full takes per script, edit together
  the best version. Don't try to nail it in one pass.

## TTS recommendations

If you're using a TTS engine instead of a human voice:

- **ElevenLabs** — best for natural conversational delivery. Pick a male
  or female voice with "narration" or "documentary" tags. Avoid the
  high-energy "commercial" voices — they sound like ad reads.
- **Azure Neural TTS** — `en-US-GuyNeural` or `en-US-AriaNeural` work well.
  Use SSML `<break time="500ms">` for the ‖ marks and `<break time="1s">`
  for ‖‖ marks.
- **OpenAI TTS** — `nova` or `onyx` voices are good for tutorials. Cheaper
  than ElevenLabs but less expressive.

The ‖ pause marks were placed for natural reading rhythm — if you're
feeding into a TTS engine, replace them with `<break>` tags or just
trust the engine's punctuation handling.
